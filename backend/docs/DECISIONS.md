# Decisions and assumptions

What was decided during the takeover where the cahier des charges was silent, impossible to follow literally, or
depended on information Diva Software has not provided yet. **Items marked "to validate" need an answer from Diva.**

## Functional decisions

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The price is entered by the platform when it activates a plan; the client's plan change is a request | The price grid is not decided (P6). No price is ever shown that Diva has not set |
| F2 | Every plan starts with the 30-day trial | One registration path; the paid plan starts when the platform activates it |
| F3 | A lapsed subscription makes the industrial account read-only (HTTP 402 on writes) instead of locking it out | The client must still reach its regulatory documents |
| F4 | Providers use the platform for free and are not subject to a subscription | Cahier: providers are invited parties |
| F5 | Stock is computed from lots, never stored | A stored counter can drift from the lots; the lots are the legal record |
| F6 | The dashboard is computed live with a 60-second cache | Simpler and exact. To revisit with measurements at volume (see OPERATIONS) |
| F7 | A pickup is flagged when its total **or any of its lots** is beyond the variance threshold | A 9 % gap on a small hazardous lot was hidden by a large lot received in full |
| F8 | Whoever may load a pickup (`pickups.load`) sees the confirmed pickups of the site, and nothing else about pickups | The operator must see what to load, not prices or history |
| F9 | Electronic signature of the slip = authenticated user + password re-entered + time + IP, in a fixed order | Meets "signed by each party" for traceability. **It is not a qualified signature — legal value to validate** |
| F10 | The annual declaration is a structured document computed from the data, validated then frozen | **The official ANGed form was not available — layout to align** |
| F11 | Offline covers lot creation and storing; loading a pickup needs the network | Loading changes what the provider sees and expects |
| F12 | Lockout: 5 failed sign-ins → 15 minutes, with an email to the account owner | The cahier gives the count, not the duration |
| F13 | Generated reports are private to the user who requested them and expire after 30 days | They are built with that user's site rights |
| F14 | Company legal name, tax ID and country are changed by the platform only | They identify the contracting party on invoices and regulatory documents |
| F16 | Zone capacity is counted in lots per packaging ("40 bales, or 100 bags"); shares add up; an optional weight limit is a second ceiling. A lot whose packaging the zone does not mention is reported, not converted | Storage room depends on volume, not weight, and people know how many bales fit, not how many m³. No guessed conversion |
| F17 | The waste types and colours a zone is reserved for are a warning, never a refusal | An operator on the floor must never be blocked from recording a lot |
| F18 | Pantone: a reference typed by the company on its own colours; the Pantone library is not bundled (see F35) | The library is proprietary; bundling it needs a licence |
| F19 | A transporter or receiver without an account is recorded by the industrial as its own partner (not a company of the platform). The industrial records the receiver's stages; a receiver with an account keeps its own | The same partner recorded by two industrials must not become one shared, unverified company; tenant isolation stays simple |
| F20 | The platform does not review the authorisation of a partner without an account; for hazardous waste a recorded reference, valid on the pickup date and marked as covering hazardous waste, is required | The industrial answers for the partners it brings. **Whether this is enough under Tunisian law is to validate** |
| F21 | Every waste has a tracking slip, issued automatically at loading; one per regulatory code, or per waste type when a non-hazardous waste has no code. Closing a pickup does not require its slips to be signed | Requested by the client. Blocking the closure on paper that may lag would stop the trail of the lots; **to confirm** |
| F22 | A party signs electronically or by hand. A slip with a handwritten signature is complete only when the scan of the signed copy is attached; that scan is the archived document | A typed-in name is not a signature: the paper is the proof |
| F23 | An authorisation (customs declaration) is a quota per waste type over a period; its usage is counted from the pickup lines, never stored. A pickup holds its share from the request, and is refused when nothing covers it on the pickup date | Requested by the client: blocked until a new authorisation is entered. Counting from the lines means a cancelled pickup releases its share by itself |
| F24 | The allowed quantity is a weight, a number of lots and/or lots per packaging — the same engine as zone capacity. At loading, a weight above the remaining room is shown as exceeded, not refused | The document states quantities in different forms. A truck already loaded cannot be un-loaded by software |
| F25 | A partner without an account is tied to an account by hand, on a suggestion based on the same tax ID among published providers. History keeps the partner as recorded | Automatic merging on a tax ID typed by someone else is not safe enough; published providers are already visible to every industrial |
| F26 | Production is a reference to attribute waste (clients, suppliers, materials and received batches, articles with consumption per piece, orders, OFs with stages per site and real consumption), not an ERP: no planning, purchasing, stock or prices | The factories already run an ERP for those; duplicating it would be large and always behind. The import feeds this reference from it |
| F27 | The import maps the columns of any file to the information needed, checks every line before writing, skips lines in error, creates on the way what a line points to, and is idempotent | No fixed template to conform to: each ERP exports differently. Re-importing a corrected file must be safe |
| F28 | New permissions `production.view`, `production.manage`, `production.stats.view`. An existing role receives a permission that is new in a version only if its default matrix includes it; roles edited by hand keep their edits | Clients and volumes are commercially sensitive. `php artisan db:seed --class=AuthorizationSeeder --force` must be run on upgrade |
| F29 | A lot's stage is deduced from the type of its zone, its material from the OF when the OF has a single one; the operator only chooses the OF | One tap on the floor. A lot created without an OF still knows its stage, which is what makes the estimate possible |
| F30 | Statistics keep measured (lot names its OF) and estimated (lot only says its stage; shared between the OFs at that stage, site and day, by pieces done) apart; what cannot be attributed is reported, not guessed | Sewing-line waste often cannot be tied to one OF. An estimate shown as a measurement would be a false precision |
| F31 | The waste rate is waste ÷ consumed material in kg; metres are converted with the fabric's width and weight, otherwise no rate is shown | Waste is weighed, fabric is measured in metres: without width and weight the two cannot be compared |
| F32 | Pieces produced per site and month follow the finished OFs (month of the end, site of the last stage); a manual entry always wins | The dashboard's kg per piece stays right without retyping; the person who typed a figure knows better than a rule |
| F33 | The word is "limite", not "seuil"; a line of stock that nothing watches says so ("Aucune limite", "Capacité non définie") and offers to define one | Client feedback: an absent limit must not look like "everything is fine" |
| F34 | Most pages open with a short explanation that can be hidden, remembered per page on the device | Client feedback: the screens must explain themselves to an occasional user |
| F35 | The application can hold a colour catalogue but ships none: `php artisan colors:import` loads JSON files the operator of the platform provides, `colors:remove-catalogue` removes them. No Pantone data is in the repository | The dataset tested (github.com/brettapeters/pantones) has no licence, and Pantone claims rights on its libraries: using it is a decision for whoever holds the rights or a licence, not something the code may assume |
| F36 | The catalogue lives in `color_families` (rows without a company, `source = import`), is read-only, and is not offered on lots: a company takes a colour into its own list, and only that list is offered | One colour table and one foreign key everywhere; operators choose in a short list, not among 3,000 swatches; removing the catalogue never breaks a lot |
| F37 | A reference is unique per owner, colour system and collection, never alone | The same reference exists in several collections with different colours |
| F38 | Lab values are computed from the HEX value (sRGB, D65); imported colours are marked `unverified`; matches use CIEDE2000 and are labelled approximate | Nothing was measured on a physical sample: the application must not present a screen colour as a colour standard |
| F39 | A company colour is deleted only while nothing uses it; otherwise it is withdrawn | Lots and documents keep naming their colour |
| F40 | Received lot, produced lot and waste lot are three records. A received lot carries its own supplier (proposed from the material, never rewritten by it); a produced lot belongs to an OF and has no supplier | The supplier is a fact about a delivery, the client a fact about an order; mixing them breaks traceability |
| F41 | Neither received nor produced lots are a stock: no balance, location or movement. "Consumed" on a received lot is the sum of what OFs declared; more than the lot held is flagged, not blocked | The cahier keeps raw-material inventory out of scope; a flag reveals a data error without stopping production records |
| F42 | Material cost = quantity consumed × cost of the received lot (price paid + delivery cost spread over its quantity). Lot not specified = weighted average of the priced lots of the material, counted apart as "estimated". No priced lot at all = unknown, never zero | Actual lot cost is the only method that needs no assumption; the average is an explicit, labelled fallback; a silent zero would overstate every margin |
| F43 | There is no invoicing module for sales (the Billing module is the platform's subscription). Selling prices sit on order lines; invoices and credit notes issued elsewhere are recorded by number, date and amount | The least disruptive way to tell "ordered" from "invoiced" without building accounting |
| F44 | Invoices belong to an OF only when they name it or when the order has a single OF; otherwise they stay at order level | Any other split would invent a figure |
| F45 | Recorded gain = produced quantity × net agreed price − recorded costs, given only when price, production and every consumption cost are known; the missing data is named | A partial margin presented as profit is worse than no margin |
| F46 | Amounts are excluding tax in the company's currency; an amount in another currency is left out and reported. No exchange rates | No rate source exists; converting silently would falsify totals |
| F47 | Money is behind `production.finance.view`: without it amounts are null in every answer, cannot be written (existing ones are kept), and money imports are refused | Workshop planners follow production without seeing prices or margins |
| F48 | Imports of events (received lots, consumptions, produced lots, expenses, invoices) require their parent to exist and never create or choose one; reference data imports still create clients/suppliers/articles and say so | A wrong parent created silently is worse than a rejected line |
| F49 | The waste register stays the one record of waste; production, received and produced lots only explain it. Navigation follows: operations, compliance, reference, then production (its waste statistics first, money last) | DivaWaste is a waste-traceability tool, not an ERP |
| F50 | A gap is judged per lot, as a percentage of what left, by the most precise rule among: waste type + channel, type, channel, every waste, then the company setting (5 % by default, cahier M4-04). No blocking limit and no regulatory percentage exist by default | The cahier defines only the 5 % warning; any other value is the company's to configure, a legal one only with its text |
| F51 | What a type is compared in follows from its unit (mass, count, volume). Pieces or litres become kilograms only through the conversion the type states; otherwise the weight is recorded as 0 kg with `weight_basis = unknown` and shown as "non mesuré" | `net_weight_kg` is not nullable across stock, statistics and documents; a flagged zero is the least disruptive way never to present a guess as a weight |
| F52 | Above the warning limit the gap is explained (pickups.complete); above the blocking limit it is accepted by pickups.authorize_variance, with a reason. Nothing is rejected or altered automatically; closing is what waits | The cahier asks for an acknowledgement, not a refusal; a blocking level needed a distinct entitlement |
| F53 | The rule and the figures it was applied to are stored on the pickup line at reception | A later change of settings must not rewrite why a decision was taken |
| F54 | A missing or zero reference gives no percentage and asks for a review; 0 against 0 is not a gap | A percentage of nothing is meaningless; hiding the case would be worse |
| F55 | The whole of a pickup is judged apart only when a weighed lot that left was not received at all | Each received lot is already judged by its own rule; with different rules per type a single total percentage has no meaning |
| F56 | Counted lots are not split or grouped, and are priced flat unless weighed | Both operations and per-kg prices share out a weight the lot does not have |
| F57 | A weight nobody measured is empty, never 0 kg. A lot may be registered, stored, labelled and collected before it is weighed; each action that needs the weight (split, group, price per kg, weight-limited authorisation) is refused with the lot and the rule named | An unknown read as zero passes limits it should not and understates every total. Blocking everything until weighing would not match the floor, where a lot is often registered at the end of a shift |
| F58 | A weight is measured, computed (conversion stated by the waste type), estimated (declared as such by the person) or unknown. An estimate is displayed but not accepted where a measure is required | An estimate presented as a weighing would be a false precision in a regulatory record |
| F59 | Totals in kilograms are the sum of the known weights, with the number of lots without one beside them; a stock limit below its ceiling with unweighed lots is "non vérifiable" | The reader must be able to tell "under the limit" from "we do not know" |
| F60 | Existing lots: only the 0 kg that was a flagged placeholder (counted waste nobody weighed) becomes empty. A 0 kg typed as a weight stays; issued documents are not rewritten | The placeholder is the only zero known not to be a measure |
| F61 | A lot with a packaging counts its packages: one by default, flagged unconfirmed until entered or corrected. A loose lot has no count and none is assumed. Existing lots with a packaging received the default of one | "One lot = one package" was what zones and authorisations counted; keeping it as a flagged default moves no figure and claims nothing nobody counted |
| F62 | Zones and authorisations count packages, not lots. Mixed packagings share one room (count ÷ capacity of that packaging, added up). Loose lots and packagings a capacity does not name are reported apart | 12 bags in one lot take 12 places; a ratio per packaging is the only capacity a workshop can state without volumes |
| F63 | An authorisation stated in weight accepts any packaging. One stated in packages only cannot be verified for loose waste or an unnamed packaging and refuses, naming the lots. Weight and packages on one document are both checked | A missing packaging line is not a prohibition; a limit that cannot be counted is not a limit that is met |
| F64 | A count is edited while the lot is on site (the pickup being prepared follows and its authorisation is asked again). After departure it is a correction with a reason: the line keeps the count it left with and stores the corrected one beside it, issued slips and the stored authorisation decision are untouched, and the allowance is drawn by the corrected count | History must show what was decided with which figure, and the allowance must reflect what really left |
| F65 | Split and group never derive package counts from the lots they come from; the operator's count is taken, else the default of one | Twelve bags split in two are not twelve and twelve |
| F66 | A departure weight that was only an estimate gives an indicative gap at reception and the outcome "no reference": the tolerance is not said to be met | An estimate cannot satisfy a rule that requires a measure (F58) |
| F67 | Each limit is answered on its own, in its own unit, with one of four results: respected, warning, exceeded, unverifiable. The percentage of one limit is never compared with, or shown for, another. `Quota` (highest percentage wins) is replaced by `Constraint` | 90 % of a weight and 103 % of a number of bags say nothing about each other; the highest percentage hid which limit failed and by how much |
| F68 | Exceeded is decided on what is known; unverifiable means known below the limit with a quantity missing. Known above the limit with a quantity missing is exceeded, and the lots are still named | Unknown is never zero, and never a met limit; an excess already proven does not become a doubt |
| F69 | The overall result comes from what each limit requires. Authorisation limits block when exceeded or unverifiable, and the refusal lists every limit with its answer. Zone room, zone load and stock limits alert and block nothing, as before | One failure must not hide another; what blocks is a rule of each operation, not of the arithmetic |
| F70 | Several packagings on one authorisation or zone ("100 bags or 50 boxes") remain one shared allowance in one dimension, shown as one limit with a line per packaging. Zone room and zone load are two limits | Agreed in phase 2 (F62); a share of the same room is a real sum, a weight and a room are not |
| F71 | The decision kept with each pickup line now holds every limit before and with the pickup (figures, unit, result). Decisions stored earlier keep their format and are not rewritten | The trail must show which limit was answered with which figure |
| F72 | On an authorisation each packaging has its own limit (300 bags and 40 bales are two limits). One shared quantity exists only when the document states it, recorded with `shared_packaging_allowance`. Supersedes F70 for authorisations; zones keep their shared room (F62). Authorisations with several packagings recorded before the flag were counted as shared and are migrated as shared | Different packagings are not one quota merely because they are packages. Migrating old documents as shared allows nothing that was refused before; they can be reviewed one by one |
| F73 | A packaged lot that does not say how many packages it is makes the limit of its packaging unverifiable; it is not counted as one. Lots no packaging limit names are a line of their own ("unlisted"): listed when the weight or the number of lots covers them, unverifiable otherwise | Unknown is neither one nor zero; the default of one is a figure the lot carries and shows, not something the check invents |
| F74 | A stock limit has a third stored state, unverifiable. People are told when the state gets worse (nothing → warning → unverifiable → exceeded) and when an unverifiable limit turns out to be at its warning once weighed; never twice for the same state, never when it falls back from exceeded. In-app and by email, like "exceeded" | Same transition rule as before, so recalculating an unchanged stock tells nobody again; a total nobody can verify needs someone to weigh, hence the email |
| F75 | A refused authorisation is explained to the user from the limits the API answered with (limit, unit, excess, lots, what to do), in the user's language | The figures already travel with the refusal; a generic sentence made people look for them elsewhere |
| F76 | The room of a zone is measured one way, stated by the zone (`space_method`): packages per packaging sharing one room, or volume against `capacity_m3`. Never both, never the higher of two. Existing zones keep the packaging way; a volume already on record is not turned into a limit by the migration | A volume and a count of packages are two measures of the same room: adding or comparing them means nothing. An explicit choice moves no existing figure |
| F77 | By volume, a packaged lot takes its packages × the volume the company states for that packaging (`packaging_volumes`); loose waste takes its measured volume, else its weight ÷ the density of its waste type. Anything else is unknown: the lot is named and the room is unverifiable (or exceeded on what is known) | Only volumes someone stated or that follow from a stated conversion are reliable; a guessed size would make the occupancy look verified |
| F78 | `sites.capacity_kg` is a watched limit ('site_load'), with its own alert percentage: every lot on the site once, whatever its zone, zones without limits included. Unweighed lots are named. Sites that already had a capacity on record start showing it | The weight of a site is not the sum of its limited zones; a lot in a zone without a limit still weighs on the site |
| F79 | Zone room, zone load and site weight alert and refuse nothing. People who see the stock of the site are notified (in-app; by email for unverifiable and exceeded, like the stock limits) when the state gets worse, or when an unverifiable limit turns out to be at its warning; never twice for the same state. The last announced state per limit is kept in `capacity_alerts`; the occupancy itself is still never stored. Re-evaluated after every stock movement and whenever a zone, a site capacity or a packaging volume is changed | Same transition rule as stock limits (F74), so an unchanged state is silent. One small table of states is the least that makes deduplication reliable; no new notification framework |
| F81 | A stock limit has a unit (`unit_code`: kg, t, piece, l, m3, or `package` with `packaging_type_id`) and a scope carries one limit per measure (`measure_key`: mass, count, volume, package:<id>). `max_quantity_kg` became `max_quantity`; existing limits are kg with the same figure. Pieces and volumes are accepted only for a waste type counted that way or stating a unit weight / a density. Lots are read by `Common\Support\Measure` with `UnitConversion` and nothing else | The restriction is often not a weight ("100 bags"). One helper reads lots for stock limits and provider minimums, so the two rules share measurement and not business logic. A unit is not editable: changing it would silently change what an alert state meant |
| F82 | A limit in packages counts the packages of one named packaging; other lots of the scope are reported as outside it (`outside_count`), never converted. A packaged lot without a count is missing, not one | "Do not count cartons as bags, nor a lot as a package" |
| F83 | What a provider asks of a collection lives on its accepted-waste lines (`min_quantity` + `min_unit_code` + `min_packaging_type_id`, `required_packaging_type_id`, `is_blocking`). It is judged on the whole pickup at request time, per line, on the lines whose waste (catalogue item, else regulatory code, else family) and planned channel match. A minimum reached on known quantities is respected whatever is missing; not reached with a missing quantity is unverifiable; an estimated weight is not counted. Default is a warning; legacy `min_quantity_kg` values became kg warnings. Provider eligibility is untouched | The minimum was displayed nowhere and enforced nowhere. It belongs to the provider, so the provider decides whether it blocks |
| F84 | A blocking requirement that fails refuses `pickups/{id}/submit` (409 `PROVIDER_REQUIREMENT_NOT_MET` / `_UNVERIFIABLE`, figures in `context`). A holder of `pickups.override_provider_requirements` (see F87) may send it with `requirement_override_reason`; the decision (rules as they stood, figures, outcome, who, why, when) is stored in `pickups.provider_requirements`, audited with the pickup, and shown to both parties. The provider still confirms or refuses | The audit trail of the pickup. The rules are deleted and recreated on every profile save, so the pickup must carry its own copy |
| F87 | Sending against a blocking requirement has its own permission, `pickups.override_provider_requirements` (site-scoped; client administrator and environment manager hold it by default). `pickups.override_eligibility` is no longer used for it: that one is labelled as passing over a provider's *eligibility* check (accreditation, partnership), was wired to nothing, and would have granted this by accident to anyone given it for that purpose — and the reverse the day it is wired. A written reason is always required; the override is its own audit event (`requirement_overridden`: pickup, author, reason, requirements that failed) beside the audited change of the pickup | Least privilege with one more permission, no more. Existing databases get it from `AuthorizationSeeder`, as for any new permission |
| F88 | A company records what a partner without an account asks in `partner_requirements` (its own tenant table: family, channel, minimum + unit + packaging counted, packaging asked for, blocking, contract reference, notes, author, last change; audited on create/update/delete). Managed with `providers.partnerships.manage`. They are judged by the same code as a provider's published rules and every answer carries `source: internal` ("entered by your company, not confirmed by the partner") against `source: provider`. Warning by default; blocking only when ticked | The minimum of such a partner is known from a contract, not from the platform. Same evaluation, different authority: the answer must say whose word it is |
| F89 | When a partner is tied to the account it opened, its `partner_requirements` are neither deleted nor merged. They keep applying to pickups sent to that provider, flagged `needs_review` until someone with `providers.partnerships.manage` keeps each one (`reviewed_at`, it stays an internal requirement) or removes it; the screen shows the provider's own published rules beside them. Editing a line counts as reviewing it | "Do not silently overwrite." Dropping them at link time would lose a contractual constraint; merging them into the provider's rules would put the company's words in the provider's mouth |
| F90 | Sign-in is limited to 5 attempts a minute per account and address, with a ceiling of 60 a minute per address (`app.login_rate_limit_per_ip`). It was 5 a minute per address for everybody | Found in the acceptance audit: a workshop behind one address, or the whole platform behind a reverse proxy, refused the sixth person signing in within a minute. The lockout of an account after 5 failures (F12) is unchanged |
| F91 | `TRUSTED_PROXIES` (config/trustedproxy.php) names the reverse proxies whose forwarded client address is believed. Empty = none | Without it, behind Nginx or Cloudflare every request carries the proxy's address: rate limits, lockout, audit log and slip signatures would record one address for everybody |
| F92 | A tracking slip names every waste type it carries ("Déchets de fil, Chutes de coupe, Rebuts de confection"), from the names kept on its pickup lines, in their order. It named only the first one | Slips are issued per regulatory code (F21) and several types share a code. The signature hash is taken at signing and never recomputed, so slips already signed keep their proof; their designation on screen and on a new print becomes complete |
| F93 | A pickup's departure and received totals are empty when no lot has a weight at that stage, and the pickup says, per stage, how many lots its total leaves out (`departure_unweighed_lots`, `received_unweighed_lots`, also in the list). The kilogram difference is empty when no weighed lot was compared | "Poids au départ 0 kg" was shown for a pickup of counted waste; after reception the lots that left unweighed were no longer mentioned (F57, F59) |
| F94 | Pickup notifications state what moved in the units of the lots, per language (`quantity`, `received`, `departure`: "1 918 kg", "150 l + 1,2 m³", "298 pièce(s)", "+ 1 lot(s) sans quantité mesurée"). The gap notification gives the number of lots outside their tolerance instead of one percentage for the pickup. Notifications already stored keep their kilogram parameters and are still read | They said "0.000 kg" for counted waste and compared pieces with kilograms (F67) |
| F95 | The CSR report (PDF and Excel) shows "non pesé" for a waste type or a site none of whose lots was weighed, and "+ N lot(s) non pesé(s)" beside a partial weight; the Excel export has a column of lots not weighed | The rows read "0 kg" for cones counted in pieces and oils in litres |
| F96 | `limits:evaluate --dry-run` runs the same evaluation inside a transaction that is rolled back, with notifications held back, and lists every message it would write (recipient, type, limit, email or not). Nothing is stored or sent | A deployment must be able to see who would be told what before the first real run |
| F85 | `limits:evaluate` (daily 06:20, and by hand after a deployment) evaluates every active site of every company: zone room and load, site weight, stock limits. It goes through the same code and the same stored states as a stock movement, so a state already announced is not announced again | Limits introduced on a site already over them were silent until the next movement. No new job or notification framework |
| F86 | Figures inside notifications are prepared once per language (`detail` French, `detail_en` English, same for `quantity`, `max_quantity`, `excess`); the email and the in-app text take the one of the reader. Stock-limit messages now carry `quantity` / `max_quantity` with their unit instead of `quantity_kg` / `max_quantity_kg`; the client still reads the old parameters of notifications already sent | A notification is written when the event happens, for readers of several languages; the request locale is the wrong one |
| F80 | The phase 4 migration leaves every zone measured in packages, including zones that already had a value in `capacity_m3`: that field was never evaluated before, so a value there is not proof of intent. The value is kept, shown as "recorded, not used" on the zone, counted per site on the Sites list, and an administrator who manages zones switches the zone to volume in its form. Nothing is erased and no zone changes model by itself | Turning an unreviewed number into a limit would raise alerts nobody asked for; hiding it would lose what someone entered. (The demo seed has no such value; the three seen in rehearsal were put there by the rehearsal.) |
| F15 | Only French ships; the language switch, English and Arabic files and right-to-left were prepared, not delivered | Cahier §1: other languages are V2. All texts are already in translation files |
